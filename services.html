TPMS Sensor Programming

The tire light that won't go off

If it's on because a tire is genuinely low, the free air check fixes it in five minutes. If it comes back on with all four at the right pressure, that's the sensor, not the tire.

Those sensors sit inside the wheel and run on a sealed battery that dies somewhere around seven to ten years. They also get damaged during careless mounting. And after a rotation or a wheel swap the system often just needs a relearn, because it no longer knows which sensor is at which corner.

We read all four, tell you which one is actually the problem, and program the system back. Worth knowing if you run a second set of wheels through the winter: that set needs its own sensors, and a relearn each time it goes on.

EV & Tesla

We work on electric vehicles.

Tire rotation, mounting and balancing on Teslas and other EVs. They're not the same job as a gas car and plenty of shops would rather not touch them.

An EV carries its battery pack under the floor, so it has to come up on the correct lift points with pucks — put a lift arm in the wrong place and you're into the pack or the rocker. They're also heavy, and they run low-rolling-resistance tires that wear faster, which makes staying on top of tire rotation matter more than it does on a normal car.

Many EV tires are lined with acoustic foam to keep road noise down, and that changes how they mount and balance. We torque to spec by hand rather than run an impact gun across the wheels.
